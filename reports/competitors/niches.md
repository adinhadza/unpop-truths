# Niches explored

| Faith and prayer | 2026-10-09 | 6.4 | Best fit for our tools, but slow growth and weak pay per view likely. Avoid 'type Amen' bait and AI Jesus images. Formula: One King James verse over a stock nature clip with soft licensed music, plus a two-sentence reflection of our own. |
| Pets and animal rescue | 2026-10-09 | 6.0 | Huge audience, but rescue stories need real footage; fake-rescue and AI scam pages are being called out. Only a pet-facts page is doable. | Nostalgia (50s to 90s America) | 2026-10-09 | 5.4 | What works is copyrighted photos of toys, TV and stars. Enter only with public-domain sources; otherwise skip. Formula: One public-domain archive photo or clip (Library of Congress, National Archives), an era-style licensed track, and a short 'who remembers this?' question. |
| Cooking and old family recipes | 2026-10-10 | 6.4 | Biggest audience and the best money signals, but the most crowded, and stock footage rarely matches a real dish. AI recipe pages are being called out and their recipes fail. Formula: One pre-1929 public-domain cookbook recipe, rewritten in our own words, ingredient stock clips, method as text cards, labelled with the cookbook and year. No AI dish photos. |
| Relationships and marriage advice | 2026-10-10 | 6.0 | Easiest to automate and closest to what Unpopular Truths already does, so a second page would mostly repeat it. Plain quote cards are what Meta's repeated-content rule targets. Formula: One named research finding about couples per Reel (study shown on screen), our own 20 to 30 second script, stock couple footage, a closing question. No invented stories. |
| Military and veterans | 2026-10-10 | 6.0 | Strong engagement and fast growth are possible, but this is the riskiest niche for a faceless page: Meta removed AI veteran-tribute pages in July 2026 and the press is tracking 'digital stolen valor'. Formula: 'On this day' military history from public archives (National Archives, DVIDS, Medal of Honor citations), source on screen, our own script. No AI people, no donation asks. |
| Gardening and homesteading | 2026-10-11 | 6.2 | Easiest and safest of today's three, with a large loyal US audience. Main risk: wrong advice or fake-looking plant images, which this community calls out, plus Meta's push-down of plain stock clips with captions. Formula: 'This week in your zone': one planting or chore tip by USDA hardiness zone, from USDA or extension facts in our own words, over a stock clip of the real plant, with licensed music. |
| True stories and history | 2026-10-11 | 6.0 | Highest engagement ceiling and public archives make it legally clean. Main risk: being lumped in with fake AI history pages, the niche journalists watch most; one invented detail can sink the page. Formula: 'On this day in America': one real Library of Congress or National Archives photo with the archive credit on screen, a 60 to 90 word sourced caption, slow pan and zoom. No AI images, no invented people. |
| Cars and classic trucks | 2026-10-11 | 5.6 | Big audience, but the appeal is the exact vehicle and stock libraries are thin on specific makes and years. Main risk: photo copyright and an expert audience that punishes errors and AI fakes. Formula: 'Truck of the day, from the archive': one public-domain period photo, year, model and three checked specs on a card, and a 'did your family own one?' question. |
