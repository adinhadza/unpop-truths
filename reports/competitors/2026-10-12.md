## Patterns worth testing

- **On the news page, storms and disasters lead.** Evidence: hurricane deaths 1,140 plays (Oct 11), tornadoes 92 (Oct 12). All 12 other Reels on those days: 0 to 19. Two days, one page, so still a lead. Test: tag each Reel as weather/disaster, politics, sport or good news for 5 days; compare median plays after 24 hours.
- **Keep Reels in the 15 to 34 second band.** Evidence: Lilach Bullock (Oct 2026): better completion in that band than over 45 s; Meta (Mar 2026): views under 5 s do not count for pay. Our average watch is 3 to 6 s. Test: 20 s and 30 s versions of the same idea; compare average watch.
- **Put the hook as text in the first frame.** Evidence: Bullock: the biggest drop is in the first seconds; first-frame text and a question work best (no numbers given). Test: half the Reels with line 1 visible at second 0; compare 3-second hold and average watch.
- **Make every Reel clearly our own.** Evidence: Meta fingerprints videos and demotes low-effort reposts (Social Media Examiner, Apr 2026); one coach saw about one third the reach for watermarked reposts. Our stock clips are licensed but not unique. Test: add our own visible element (consistent frame, our wording on screen, a short voice line) to half the Reels.
- **Answer every comment in the first hour.** Evidence: Bullock's advice, and Fanpage Karma (2026) says comment quality now weighs more. Test: only once comments appear; log reply times.

## Platform notes

- Meta's 'True Interest' survey asks viewers to rate a Reel's match to their interests from 1 to 5; low ratings count against a Reel even when watch time looks fine (Social Media Examiner, Apr 2026).
- Facebook favors content made by the page itself and fingerprints videos to see who uploaded first; reposts without added value are pushed down (same source).
- Meta paid creators nearly $3 billion in 2025, 60% of it for Reels; views under 5 seconds and repeat views do not count (Social Media Today, Mar 30 2026).
